# The last ten years do most of the work

## Hook
Your money's last ten years can out-earn its first twenty. [3]
Visual: compare | Growth on ₹1 lakh at 12% | Years 1–20: ₹8.6 lakh | Years 21–30: ₹20.3 lakh

## Stakes
Same one lakh. Same rate. Only time changed.
So the years you skip at the start, or cut at the end, cost you the most.
Visual: text | The last years pay the most

## How it works
Here's why. You earn returns on your returns. [1]
Year one, twelve percent of one lakh is twelve thousand rupees.
Year thirty, the same twelve percent lands on almost twenty-seven lakh. That's over three lakh, in one year. [3]
Visual: compare | What 12% adds in one year | Year 1: ₹12,000 | Year 30: ₹3,21,000

## Shortcut
Quick check: divide seventy-two by your rate. Seventy-two by twelve is six.
Your money roughly doubles every six years. [2]
Visual: stat | 6 years | to double at 12% | Rule of 72: 72 ÷ your rate

## The doublings
Two lakh. Four. Eight. Sixteen. Then about thirty.
Each doubling adds more than all the earlier ones combined. [3]
Visual: bars | ₹1 lakh at 12% (₹ lakh) | Yr 6: 2 | Yr 12: 3.9 | Yr 18: 7.7 | Yr 24: 15.2 | Yr 30: 30

## The catch
Honest catch: twelve percent is an example, not a promise. Real returns swing year to year. [3]
But the shape holds. Slow, then sudden.
Visual: text | Slow, then sudden

## Payoff
So the boring early years aren't wasted. They're the runway.
Quit early, and you miss the steep part.
Visual: text | Don't quit before the steep part

## End
Because your money's best decade is its last.
Visual: end | Slow, then sudden | Follow for one money idea a day
