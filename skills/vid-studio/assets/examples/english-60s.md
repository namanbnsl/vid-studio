# The last ten years do most of the work

## Hook
One lakh at twelve percent earns more in its last ten years than in its first twenty. [3]
Visual: compare | What ₹1 lakh earns at 12% | Years 1–20: ₹8.6 lakh | Years 21–30: ₹20.3 lakh
Source: Worked example, 12% a year

## How it works
The reason is boring: every year's return is added to the balance, so next year's twelve percent is worked out on a bigger number. [1]
Visual: list | Why it speeds up | The year's return is added | Next year's 12% is on more | Thirty times over

## A rule you can use
To feel the pace without a calculator, divide seventy-two by the rate. At twelve percent you get six, so the money doubles about every six years. [2]
Visual: stat | 6 years | Time to double at 12% | Rule of 72: divide 72 by the rate
Source: Rule of 72 approximation

## The doublings
Six years gets you two lakh, twelve gets you four, and by thirty you're at about thirty. The last doubling alone adds fifteen lakh, more than all the earlier ones combined. [3]
Visual: bars | ₹1 lakh, doubling every 6 years | Year 6: ₹2 lakh | Year 12: ₹3.9 lakh | Year 18: ₹7.7 lakh | Year 24: ₹15.2 lakh | Year 30: ₹30 lakh

## The fine print
None of this is a forecast. I picked twelve percent to keep the maths clean, there's no fee or tax in it, and real markets don't rise in a straight line. [3]
Visual: text | Assumes 12% *every year*, no fees, no tax

## What to do with it
For a real number, open a compound interest calculator and try eight percent. One lakh becomes about four point seven lakh in twenty years, and ten in thirty. [4]
Visual: compare | ₹1 lakh at 8% a year | After 20 years: ₹4.7 lakh | After 30 years: ₹10.1 lakh
Source: Investor.gov compound interest calculator

## End
So if you're five years into an SIP and bored, you're still in the slow part.
