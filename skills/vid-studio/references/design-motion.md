# Design and motion for vertical explainers

Read for setup style samples, storyboarding, and composition authoring. Apply the approved project preference snapshot, including brand/layout/chart/asset/motion/caption rules, before these suggested techniques; user choices override aesthetic defaults. Design decisions are approved before asset production; rendering fidelity cannot rescue unclear explanation.

## Establish a visual language

Translate the profile-selected brand brief into its frame reference, then freeze the approved references under project context/ and copy that frame into the Hyperframes project. Default paths are brand/brand.md and brand/frame.md; different brands can use separate local paths. Treat its machine-readable frontmatter as tokens and its prose as composition/motion constraints. Preserve the original brand reference. The template [frame.md](../assets/workspace/brand/frame.md) gives a reviewable starting structure; do not silently approve its proposed values.

During setup, propose a coherent direction and review representative portrait title, mechanism, and chart style frames. Use the user's references when available. An editorial, diagram-led style is a useful candidate, not a universal mandated aesthetic. Freeze palette roles, typography, caption/source-label hierarchy, spacing, stroke weight, shape language, and allowed transition families. Validate language glyphs and safe areas for the actual destination.

Design directly for 1080×1920 (or approved alternative): stacked comparisons, vertical processes, large focal evidence, and deliberate negative space. Do not scale down a landscape slide or copy dashboard card chrome. Starting headline sizes around 72–104 px and key labels/captions around 42–56 px can guide portrait samples; phone-size review decides readability. Reduce content before shrinking type. Avoid generic gradients, pill badges, excessive rounded cards, and decorative stock footage unless they serve the approved direction.

Keep one dominant explanatory focus per beat. A quiet background and a stable chart can be excellent. Do not require ambient motion, multiple simultaneous focal objects, or a fixed element count to look polished.

## Choose visual form by the question

- Mechanism/causality: persistent nodes and connections, with the current relationship revealed as narration explains it. Distinguish association from causation.
- Comparison: aligned bars, stacked portrait panels, or before/after states with consistent scale and labels.
- Change over time: timelines or measured series with honest time spacing and missing-data treatment.
- Magnitude: one readable number with units and denominator, then a defensible comparison.
- Geography: baked local map geometry with attribution and sufficient geographic context.
- Real-world evidence: actual images, reports, or footage with provenance; generated illustrations remain illustrations.
- Hook/takeaway: intentional typography that introduces the question or consolidates the explanation.

Use a central visual argument across related scenes. Scene changes should signal a changed idea, not restart the layout for every sentence. The second scene should begin delivering the promised explanation; avoid stretching the hook through repeated teasers.

## Motion grammar

For every motion, specify subject, purpose, initial state, resolved state, cue, duration, and readable hold. Develop a few consistent moves:

- Reveal the next node on the spoken concept; draw a connector on the relationship.
- Reveal chart marks while axes/labels stay anchored, then hold exact values.
- Highlight the relevant part without moving the entire diagram.
- Preserve object identity across a state change; avoid replacing it with an unrelated crossfade.
- Use restrained cuts, masks, or short fades to hand attention between ideas.

Start with brief emphasis/entrance motions and smooth non-bouncy easing; tune durations to narration and complexity rather than imposing one timing on every scene. Hold final evidence long enough to read. Avoid overshoot on quantitative marks, synthetic bar races from static snapshots, background breathing, arbitrary drift, and constant camera movement. Animated intermediate chart values are reveals, not newly sourced observations.

Choreograph caption changes with spoken phrases, not animation spectacle. Protect chart labels and source notes from the caption band. Reserve only destination-approved safe areas; an upstream bottom-band convention is not universal.

## Storyboard proof

Every scene's reviewed storyboard contains its claim IDs, explanatory purpose, portrait keyframe, data encoding, motion sequence and hold, intended narration/captions, source note, and asset provenance plan. Chart scenes include a static end-state preview with approved real values. Synthetic values used only for early setup style samples must be visibly labeled illustrative and never enter the episode data package.

For production, test entrance, intermediate, settled, and ending states at arbitrary seeks, including reverse seeks when the diagnostics support them. State continuity and final-frame behavior matter as much as the entrance. Don't reset to black or to an unfinished pose at the tail unless that ending was approved.
